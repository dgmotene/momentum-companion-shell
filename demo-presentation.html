02 · The problem we chose

Two very different people, one insurance product

Halfway through the first weekend we made a call that shaped everything: we would not build for one persona. We would build for two, and the difference between them would be the whole design.

Persona A

Thandi, the policyholder

Managing her cover. Wants clarity, control, honest numbers, no fluff.

  • "What does my policy actually cover?"
  • "What if I drop my sum assured?"
  • "I've moved. Update my address."
Persona B

Sipho, the bereaved beneficiary

His mother has just passed. Not a customer with a form to fill. A person who needs a hand.

  • "How do I even start a claim?"
  • "What documents do I need?"
  • "What happens next?"
The design insight that fell out of this: the same product must feel like two products. That is when we stopped drawing a chatbot and started drawing Lumina.

The role-to-capability matrix

07 · The words we wrote by hand

The lines the AI is never allowed to improvise

A few moments carry disproportionate weight. Those lines are human-written, fixed, and reviewed as copy — not as prompts.

Opening a death claim
"I'm so sorry for your loss. Take all the time you need. I'll walk this through with you, one step at a time."
Fixed opener. Never generated. Never A/B tested. Read out loud, unchanged, every time.
The honesty fallback
"I can't confirm that from the provided documents. I can explain what is available or refer this for human review."
Word for word from the brief. When retrieval finds nothing, this replaces the model call entirely.
Refusing a beneficiary's What-If
"That action isn't available on your profile. I can help you with questions about the policy and with a claim."
Never mentions "you don't have permission." Never explains the machinery. Redirects to what is available.
Estimate disclaimer
"Estimate · A consultant confirms the final figure."
Appended in code, after the model finishes speaking. Cannot be phrased away, cannot be dropped by a rushed turn.
07 · Interaction patterns

Six patterns that make Lumina feel present, not pushy

Small choices, made deliberately. Each one earns its keep on stressful days.

○
Ask for the missing, not the missed

The assistant tracks what has been given, not what remains. Nothing is ever asked twice, even after a token refresh.

✓
Confirm before write

Every low-risk change is read back in full before it commits. High-risk changes never commit in-chat at all.

→
The human is always one tap

"Speak to a person" is a chip on every screen, not a last resort. Standing offer, no reason required.

☷
One question at a time

Grief and admin do not multi-task. Short sentences. No exclamation marks. No cheer.

№
Every amount, classified

Recorded, illustrative, potentially payable, confirmed. No figure is displayed without one of these four tags.

✦
Pause without penalty

Progress lives in the backend, not the transcript. Come back tomorrow, next week — the claim resumes exactly where it stood.

08 · The wins

Three things we're proudest of

Not the framework we chose. Not the model we picked. These.

  • 01
    The role gate lives between two model calls
    The AI never gets to grant access. A beneficiary asking for a What-If is refused by code after the classify call and before the compose call — the second call never happens. A prompt injection can change the wording of a reply, but it cannot change what the system does.
  • 02
    The bereaved never re-supply the death certificate
    The one document a grieving person dreads producing is the one we never ask for. The deceased flag — already set by Momentum — tells the checklist it's on file. This is one field that changes everything, and it is the whole soul of the product.
  • 03
    Every money figure carries its own classification
    The Scope Change brief forbids the assistant from guaranteeing outcomes. Rather than trust the model to phrase carefully, we made the type system refuse to render a value without one of four classes: recorded, illustrative, potentially payable, confirmed. Compliance in the compiler, not the prompt.
09 · Finals plan

What we build first at the finals

The design round proved the shape. The finals prove the shape holds under real gateway credentials, the full guide, and a live audience.

DAY 1 · MORNING Wire the gateway client OAuth2 client-credentials, retry with exponential backoff, both endpoints hitting real Bedrock.
DAY 1 · AFTERNOON Two-call orchestrator Classify-then-act live end to end. Grounded Q&A working against a small guide slice.
DAY 1 · EVENING Ingest the full guide Parent-child chunker over the 454-page Myriad guide. Commit the seed so it survives restarts.
DAY 2 · MORNING Claims flow end to end State machine live, SLA lookups live, deceased-flag preload proven with a real claim.
DAY 2 · AFTERNOON Streaming + polish Streaming responses, one more accessibility pass, video capture for the demo.
DAY 2 · EVENING Rehearse the demo Two dry runs of the full walkthrough. If anything breaks, we cut, not push.
